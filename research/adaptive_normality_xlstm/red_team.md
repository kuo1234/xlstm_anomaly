| # | Attack on the candidate | Why it can invalidate the story | Risk class | Evidence or scope response |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **xLSTM may be unnecessary.** A matched LSTM, GRU, SSM, simple autoregression or moving statistic may perform as well. | The current rationale invokes a recurrent architecture but no adaptation task shows xLSTM is required. G1's post-hoc matched LSTM has a comparable internal-state signal. | **NOVELTY_THREAT** | M1/M3/M4 require matched controls. If xLSTM does not win a predeclared practical margin, reframe as an architecture-neutral transfer/adaptation study. |
| 2 | **Persistent state may worsen contamination.** A transient or persistent fault can alter state, after which it is predicted as normal. | A low post-onset residual could reflect self-training on the anomaly, not benign acceptance. Freezing state does not remove suspicious samples already in recent context. | **RESOLVABLE_BY_EXPERIMENT** | M3/M5 need pre-update scoring, explicit state-write logs, contamination exposure, reset/freeze/shadow/rollback comparisons, and score decay through long events. |
| 3 | **Multi-horizon forecasts may duplicate moving statistics.** | A long forecast residual or horizon maximum may mostly indicate volatility/trend and fail to improve event detection. ICLR 2026 one-liners found simple moving-window baselines competitive with TS foundation models in its tested settings. | **RESOLVABLE_BY_EXPERIMENT** | M1/M2 compare last value, moving variance/difference, linear forecast, xLSTM/LSTM and reconstruction with point/segment timing. |
| 4 | **Normal/quarantine memory may duplicate CANDI, M2N2, MemTTA or drift pools.** | The novelty may be a relabeling or composition of existing selective TTA, normal memory, quarantine and model-pool mechanisms. ACM E-Energy 2026 already separates normal / normal drift / attack and selectively updates on the first two with a small labeled anchor set. | **NOVELTY_THREAT** | M0 requires a claim-by-claim matrix; compare against CANDI, M2N2, the E-Energy method and the appropriate pool baseline. A new module is not a contribution without a behavior prior methods lack and a test proving it. |
| 5 | **A stable persistent anomaly may look exactly like a new normal.** | If benign mode and fault induce the same observed distribution and no external context, no observation-only classifier can distinguish them. | **FATAL_IF_TRUE** | For automatic safe promotion, require independent mode/fault context or an explicit accepted-risk policy. Without it, output an unclassified persistent shift and do not promote. This is fatal to the unconditional observation-only safety claim. |
| 6 | **Confirmed-normal target data may be unrealistic.** | Operations teams may not have a clean target prefix; it may be costly to obtain, delayed, or contaminated. | **SCOPING_LIMITATION** | Problem A must report annotation/confirmation source and sample/time cost. If no trusted target-normal data exist, scope to a different unsupervised TTA problem and re-evaluate separately. |
| 7 | **Adaptation may catastrophically forget prior regimes.** | Tuning on B can reduce detection or raise alarms when the stream returns to A. | **RESOLVABLE_BY_EXPERIMENT** | M5/M6 include A→B→A, old-regime retention, per-update snapshots and rollback/pool alternatives. |
| 8 | **Different machines may have incompatible channel semantics.** | Same dimension count does not mean same variable identity, ordering, unit or dependency structure. SMD's D=38 is convenient but does not prove aligned channels. | **SCOPING_LIMITATION** | Keep the first claim same-D with documented channel mappings. Treat arbitrary-D or semantic mismatch as a later independent problem. |
| 9 | **Preprocessing may dominate the detector, as in R0.** | Scaling and near-constant channels can generate large score effects or degenerate thresholds, obscuring any recurrent contribution. | **RESOLVABLE_BY_EXPERIMENT** | Reuse per-machine scale/variance checks; freeze scaling policy before labels; report raw/normalized and threshold behavior, per-channel degeneracies and per-machine results. Never repair a result after seeing it. |
| 10 | **Aggregate metrics may hide machine-level failure.** | A pooled AP can be driven by one machine or event type and conceal catastrophic false positives elsewhere. | **RESOLVABLE_BY_EXPERIMENT** | Make held-out-machine macro and individual curves primary; cluster by machine/source/event; publish worst-case and distribution, not only pooled scores. |
| 11 | **The R0 negative result may mean recurrent state is not useful.** | R0 found no resolved state increment for either recurrent backbone. Although it reset state every W64 window, the broader motivation might still be weak. | **RESOLVABLE_BY_EXPERIMENT** | Do not reinterpret R0 as a persistent-state test. M3 is the narrow missing test; if persistent state adds no stream utility beyond rolling observables, drop it. |
| 12 | **The full complexity may lose to trend/change-point plus a standard detector.** | State machines, shadow models, quarantine, multi-horizon predictors and regime pools may add latency and failure modes without improvement. | **FATAL_IF_TRUE** | Compare the smallest proposed system with a simple change-point detector plus fixed LSTM/AE/forecast detector. If complexity gives no meaningful reduction in false-positive persistence with preserved fault detection, do not build the integrated system. |

## Hardest unresolved risk

The system can learn that a stable shift is statistically coherent. It cannot infer whether an operator authorized that shift when the same signals are compatible with a persistent failure or attack. Persistence, predictability, density, uncertainty and cross-channel agreement help describe the shift, but do not supply intent. Any system that automatically promotes on those signals must be evaluated as a risk policy, not presented as semantic identification.
